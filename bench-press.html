BENCH PRESS

CHEST • TRICEPS • FRONT DELTS

A proven upper-body pressing movement for building strength, muscle and pushing power. The goal is not just moving more weight — it is moving it with control.

Goal
Strength + Muscle
Level
Beginner–Advanced
Equipment
Barbell / Smith
Primary Muscle
Chest

HOW TO DO IT

1

Set Up

Lie on the bench with your eyes roughly under the bar. Plant your feet firmly and pull your shoulder blades back and down.

2

Grip

Grip the bar slightly wider than shoulder width. Keep your wrists stacked over your forearms.

3

Unrack

Brace your trunk and bring the bar out over your chest without losing your upper-back position.

4

Lower

Lower the bar under control toward the mid-to-lower chest. Keep your elbows from flaring straight out to the sides.

5

Press

Drive the bar upward while keeping your feet planted, upper back tight and shoulders controlled.

Muscles Worked

Primary & Secondary

  • Chest
    Primary pressing muscle
  • Triceps
    Assists elbow extension
  • Front Delts
    Assist shoulder flexion and pressing
BUILT50 Setup

Make Every Rep Cleaner

  • Grip:
    Slightly wider than shoulder width for most lifters.
  • Shoulders:
    Pull the shoulder blades back and down against the bench.
  • Feet:
    Keep them planted and stable throughout the set.
  • Bar Path:
    Lower toward the mid-to-lower chest and press back toward the start.

COMMON MISTAKES

Excessive Arch

A normal arch is fine. Turning the movement into a full-body bridge just to shorten the range is usually not the goal.

Elbows Flaring Too Wide

Letting the elbows shoot straight out can put unnecessary stress on the shoulders for some lifters.

Bouncing the Bar

Lower under control. Do not use your chest as a trampoline to create momentum.

SETS & REPS

Strength
3–5 sets • 3–6 reps

Heavier loads with longer rest periods. Technique still comes first.

Muscle Building
3–4 sets • 6–12 reps

A practical range for accumulating quality pressing volume.

Higher Rep Work
2–4 sets • 12–20 reps

Best suited to controlled technique and lighter loading.

JOINT-FRIENDLY OPTIONS

Dumbbell Bench Press

Allows each arm to move independently and may feel more natural for some shoulders.

Incline Press

Changes the pressing angle and shifts more work toward the upper chest.

Smith Machine Press

Adds stability and can make loading and setup more predictable.

Neutral-Grip Dumbbells

A palms-facing grip can feel more comfortable for lifters with irritated shoulders.

BUILT50 Coaching Notes
The bench press is a tool, not a religion.

If your shoulders hate it, change the grip, range, angle or implement. Your chest does not know whether the resistance came from a barbell, dumbbells or a machine. The goal is to train the muscle effectively and keep progressing.
