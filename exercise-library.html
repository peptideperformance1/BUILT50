CHEST

PRESS. STRETCH. CONTROL.

Chest training doesn't require ten versions of the same press. Use stable pressing movements, add a good lengthened position and progress what you can perform comfortably.

Compound Press

Bench Press

A basic horizontal press for chest, triceps and anterior delts. Use a grip and range of motion that allow controlled reps without forcing irritated shoulders.

Primary: Chest
Compound Press

Incline Dumbbell Press

Useful for emphasizing the upper chest while allowing each arm to move independently. Keep the incline moderate rather than turning it into a shoulder press.

Primary: Upper Chest
Stable Press

Machine Chest Press

A strong option when you want to push hard without spending energy stabilizing a bar or dumbbells. Particularly useful for higher-rep hypertrophy work.

Primary: Chest
Isolation

Cable Fly

Keeps tension on the chest through a large range of motion. Control the stretch and bring the arms together without turning the movement into a press.

Primary: Chest
BACK

WIDTH. THICKNESS. CONTROL.

A complete back routine usually includes both vertical pulling and rowing. Don't turn every back exercise into a competition to see how far you can move the weight stack.

Vertical Pull

Neutral-Grip Lat Pulldown

A shoulder-friendly vertical pulling option that trains the lats while also involving the upper back and biceps.

Primary: Lats
Row

Chest-Supported Row

Removes much of the lower-back demand and lets you concentrate on pulling through the elbows and training the upper back hard.

Primary: Upper Back
Row

Seated Cable Row

A versatile horizontal pull. Keep the torso controlled and finish by driving the elbows back rather than leaning away from the stack.

Primary: Mid Back
Lat Isolation

Straight-Arm Pulldown

Trains shoulder extension without much elbow flexion, making it a useful way to work the lats without the biceps taking over.

Primary: Lats
SHOULDERS

BUILD THE FRAME.

Side and rear delts do a lot for the look of a developed upper body. They also respond well to controlled repetitions rather than throwing the heaviest dumbbells in the rack around.

Isolation

Cable Lateral Raise

Provides consistent resistance through the movement and makes it easy to train one side at a time with controlled technique.

Primary: Side Delts
Isolation

Dumbbell Lateral Raise

Simple and effective. Lead with the elbows, avoid excessive momentum and don't confuse lifting higher with lifting better.

Primary: Side Delts
Rear Delt

Reverse Pec Deck

A stable way to train the rear delts and upper back. Keep the arms moving through a controlled arc instead of jerking the handles backward.

Primary: Rear Delts
Rear Delt

Cable Rear-Delt Fly

Allows steady tension and easy adjustment of the line of pull. Use lighter loads and keep the movement at the shoulder.

Primary: Rear Delts
ARMS

CURL. EXTEND. DON'T OVERCOMPLICATE IT.

Arms already work during presses, rows and pulldowns. Direct work fills the gaps. A couple good curl and extension variations performed consistently will go a long way.

Biceps

Incline Dumbbell Curl

Places the biceps in a lengthened position and makes it difficult to cheat if the upper arm stays behind the torso.

Primary: Biceps
Biceps / Forearms

Hammer Curl

Uses a neutral grip and heavily involves the brachialis and brachioradialis alongside the biceps.

Primary: Biceps & Brachialis
Triceps

Rope Pressdown

A reliable triceps movement that's easy to load and control. Keep the elbows relatively fixed and extend fully without turning it into a full-body exercise.

Primary: Triceps
Triceps

Overhead Cable Extension

Trains the triceps with the arm overhead and places the long head in a more lengthened position. Use a comfortable cable path.

Primary: Triceps
LEGS

BUILD THE BASE.

Strong legs matter for more than filling out a pair of shorts. Train the quads, hamstrings, glutes and calves through movement patterns you can progressively load.

Squat Pattern

Squat

One of many useful ways to train the quads and glutes. Choose a stance and depth you can control while maintaining a strong, repeatable position.

Primary: Quads & Glutes
Machine Compound

Leg Press

Provides a stable way to train the legs hard with less demand on balance and torso stabilization. Control the bottom position.

Primary: Quads & Glutes
Hip Hinge

Romanian Deadlift

Trains the hamstrings and glutes through a loaded hip hinge. Push the hips back and stop when your available hamstring range ends rather than chasing the floor.

Primary: Hamstrings & Glutes
Hamstrings

Leg Curl

Direct knee-flexion work for the hamstrings. Control both the shortening and lowering portions instead of letting the stack slam between reps.

Primary: Hamstrings
Quads

Leg Extension

Direct quadriceps training that works well for moderate and higher repetitions. Use a controlled range that feels good on your knees.

Primary: Quadriceps
Calves

Calf Raise

Whether seated or standing, use a controlled stretch at the bottom and finish each repetition through the ankle rather than bouncing.

Primary: Calves
CORE

STRONG THROUGH THE MIDDLE.

Core training isn't just endless crunches. Use movements that train trunk flexion, stability and resistance to unwanted movement.

Flexion

Cable Crunch

Allows progressive resistance for the abdominal muscles. Think about bringing the ribs toward the pelvis instead of simply pulling the rope downward.

Primary: Abdominals
Stability

Plank

A basic anti-extension exercise. Maintain a strong position from shoulders through hips instead of chasing the longest possible hold with poor posture.

Primary: Trunk Stability
Anti-Rotation

Pallof Press

Trains the trunk to resist rotation while the cable tries to pull you sideways. Keep the hips and shoulders square.

Primary: Core Stability
Loaded Carry

Farmer Carry

Trains grip, trunk stability and total-body bracing while walking under load. Stay tall and control each step.

Primary: Core & Grip
BUILT50 PRINCIPLE

The best exercise isn't the one that looks impressive online. It's the one you can perform well, progressively load and keep using long enough to produce a result.
